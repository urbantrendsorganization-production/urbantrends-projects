NOTE — ACCENT DISCIPLINE
Clay is reserved for exactly one thing per screen: the action that moves the booking forward. Money that has already been paid is green; money still owed is neutral. If two clay buttons appear on one screen, one of them is wrong.
NOTE — M-PESA GREEN
Safaricom green appears only at the payment moment — the STK screen, the deposit badge, the paid amount. Using it as a general success colour would spend its equity on toasts about saved settings.

5 · STK WAITING — the hand-off screen
NOTE — WHY THIS SCREEN GOES DARK
This is the only screen in BookNasi that changes its whole background. The client is about to leave the browser for the SIM toolkit; when they come back they need to recognise instantly that they are still in the middle of the same payment. A dark, green-lit screen is a landmark. Everything else on it — steps, timer, resend — exists so they never have to guess whether the payment worked.
NOTE — THE HELD SLOT IS THE PROMISE
The slot sits in pending_payment with a short TTL and a countdown the client can see. The countdown is not decoration — it is the reason it's safe to ask someone to leave the page. When it expires the server releases the slot, and this screen must say so before the client finds out at the shop.
NOTE — *334# ESCAPE HATCH
On a loaded 3G network the STK push sometimes never surfaces. Naming the USSD fallback in step 2 recovers those bookings instead of losing them to a silent wait.

STAFF TODAY — one screen, no navigation, walk-in always reachable
NOTE — NOW / NEXT / DONE
The list is not a calendar. It is three bands: what's in the chair, what's coming, what's finished — finished sinks to the bottom in grey. A stylist glancing down mid-braid needs one thing in colour, and that thing is the appointment they're inside.
NOTE — NO NAV BAR
There is no tab bar, no hamburger, no dashboard for staff. Everything that isn't today's list or a walk-in lives behind the avatar. Nested navigation is how this screen loses to paper.
NOTE — DEPOSITS ARE VISIBLE TO STAFF
The green badge on each row is the only reason staff trust an online booking enough to keep the chair free. It is also why the deposit total sits in the header: it is their earnings, already banked.
NOTE — OFFLINE IS NORMAL
Walk-ins write locally first and sync when signal returns; the row appears immediately with a pending dot. A staff action must never be blocked on the network — that is the one thing the notebook does perfectly.

NOTE — WHAT NEVER TOKENISES
Four things stay fixed however the host restyles it: the 52px target height, the three-per-row slot grid, the visible hold countdown, and the *334# fallback line. They are the difference between a payment that completes and one that doesn't, so they are not theme.

NOTE — THE LINK IS THE SESSION
No accounts means the SMS link carries a signed, single-booking token: it opens this booking and nothing else, expires after the appointment, and never exposes another client's data. If the link is lost, re-sending it to the same number is the only recovery — never a lookup by phone number alone.
NOTE — MOVE BEATS CANCEL
On both sheets rescheduling is the clay button and cancelling is the quiet one, because a moved booking keeps the chair filled and the deposit intact. Inside 24 hours this is the only outcome that's good for both sides, so it is stated as such rather than buried.
NOTE — REFUND IS A PROMISE WITH A TIME
B2C M-Pesa payouts aren't instant. Every refund message states a window and promises a confirming SMS; silence after a cancel is what generates the WhatsApp complaint.
